Privacy

Nothing leaves your phone. Not by default, not by exception.

Health data is uniquely sensitive. This is a hard architectural constraint — there's no server to send it to, no account to log into, and no setting that changes that.

What this app never does

Not a policy — an absence

No servers

Every score, chart, and poster is computed on your iPhone. There's no backend for your data to reach.

No accounts

No sign-up, no login, no profile stored anywhere but your own device.

No analytics SDKs

No usage tracking, no third-party crash-reporting service watching how you use the app.

No ad trackers

No ad network, no cross-app identifiers, nothing about you to target or sell.

No background uploads

Background refresh exists to keep scores current while you sleep — never to send anything out.

What you control

The only things that ever leave, you send yourself

HealthKit permissions

Granted per data type at onboarding — visible and revocable any time in iOS Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices.

Sharing a poster or session card

Tapping Share hands a rendered image to the iOS share sheet — never raw health data — and you choose where it goes.

Backup & restore

Settings → Your data saves one backup file (gym log, motivation scores, fuel log, saved places, settings) wherever you choose — iCloud Drive or Files. No Apple Health data goes in it; that stays in Apple Health.

Apple Health, exactly

What TONOS reads, and the two things it writes

iOS asks you per type, and TONOS works with whatever you allow. It reads these to compute readiness, load and trends — on the iPhone, and nowhere else:

Workouts
workouts and their GPS routes, workout effort scores
Heart
heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, heart-rate recovery, VO₂ max
Sleep & recovery
sleep, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, sleeping wrist temperature, breathing disturbances, mindful minutes, time in daylight
Running & cycling
running power, speed, stride length, vertical oscillation, ground contact time; cycling power, cadence, speed, functional threshold power
Activity
steps, active and resting energy, exercise minutes, walking/running, cycling and swimming distance
Body
weight, body fat, lean body mass, height, date of birth, sex
Check-in
your mood (State of Mind) and the symptoms on your check-in list, plus fever

Your daily check-in

The mood and symptoms you log are saved as Apple Health's own State of Mind and symptom records — Apple Health is their only home.

GPX routes you import

A ride or walk you import (for example from Komoot) is saved as a workout with its route and distance.

On the phone

What stays with TONOS itself

In TONOS's own storage

Your gym log, the 1–5 motivation score, the fuel log, saved map places and your settings. They are deleted with the app.

Location — only while in use

Used only to centre the routes map on where you are. Never stored, never in the background.

Notifications — local only

Milestones and the Sunday weekly wrap are composed and scheduled on the iPhone. No push server is involved.

Apple's own services

Maps in TONOS are Apple Maps: the map tiles, and any place name you type into the search, go to Apple the way they do in the Maps app — your routes and health data do not. If you test TONOS through TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports and the feedback you choose to send with the developer, under Apple's TestFlight terms. TONOS adds no analytics of its own.

A trade-off, not a superlative

Most training and coaching apps sync your workouts to a company's servers, compute your scores there, and send them back — a reasonable trade if you want a social feed or cross-device sync. This app makes the opposite trade: every score is computed on the phone that recorded it, in exchange for staying single-device — no web dashboard, no companion service, no account to recover if you lose the phone. For training guidance built from health data, that trade felt worth making.

Not a medical device

This app gives training guidance, not a diagnosis. If a metric looks alarming — a resting heart rate that keeps climbing, for instance — that's a cue to see a clinician, not just a notification to dismiss.
