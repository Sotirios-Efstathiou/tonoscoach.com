Heat Art Studio

Every route you've logged, turned into art.

Seven named styles, five data-driven color modes, four caption layouts, and one-tap generators for a year, a trip, or a single hard effort — all rendered from GPS data that never leaves your phone.

Seven styles

One tap, a completely different poster

Style sets the whole look at once — palette, background, caption layout, and rendering engine — and stays fully editable from there.

Ember

The original look — warm glow lines over a dimmed map. Every poster starts here.

Neon City

High-contrast glow lines on pure black, like a city seen from orbit.

Blueprint

A schematic technical drawing — thin lines on a dark grid, Museum caption by default.

Topo

Routes redrawn as elevation contour bands, traced with marching squares.

Ink

A light paper poster — dark linework on off-white, built to be printed.

Thermal

Routes dissolve into a dense heat field instead of discrete lines.

Constellation

Routes as starfields — small glowing points instead of solid strokes.

Rendering engines

Three ways to draw a route

Glow lines

Each route stroked and lit with a soft additive glow — Ember, Neon City, Blueprint, Ink, and Constellation all run this engine, just with different palettes and backgrounds.

Heat density

Routes accumulate into a continuous density field instead of discrete lines — Thermal's look, and the original toggle Ember can still reach.

Contour bands

Topo runs its own pipeline: a marching-squares contour trace turns route density into elevation-style bands.

Generators

Three one-tap starting points

Year in Heat

Appears automatically when the current or previous year has routes — opens the studio already framed to that year's activities, titled, in the Museum layout.

Trip

Pick a date range and which activity types count; the match count updates live. When the matched routes carry altitude data, an elevation ridgeline option appears.

Hero

A "Make poster" button on any single workout — one tap gives you Ember style, Race layout, and that workout's headline stat, titled with the activity and date.

Independent controls

Every axis is its own dial

Caption layout

CleanTitle + one stats line, bottom-left. The original caption.
MuseumTracked uppercase title, a hairline rule, stats, and a small lat/lon coordinate line.
RaceOne dominant stat with a small title above it — built for a single hard effort.
GridSmall-multiple glyphs instead of one map, sharing Clean's caption.

Color mode

HeatThe palette's heat/glow ramp — the default.
ActivityEach route colored by activity type, with a legend when more than one appears.
YearEach route colored by the year it happened.
ElevationEach segment colored by altitude along the route.
PaceEach segment colored by pace along the route.

Style, layout, and color mode are independent — pick Blueprint with a Race layout colored by Pace if that's the combination you want. Background (Dark map, Black, Satellite, Minimal) is its own fourth dial on top.

Animated export

Routes that draw themselves on

An 8-second silent video: the map fades in, every route draws on in chronological order, the heat bloom accumulates as they finish, and the caption fades in last. Rendered frame by frame on-device and saved as a shareable MP4.

The recreation alongside this loops continuously so you can see the motion — the real export renders once, at full resolution, to your camera roll.

Formats

From a phone wallpaper to a wall print

Wallpaper renders at your exact device resolution as a lock-screen/home-screen preset. Print formats export as a 300 DPI PDF, ready to send to a print shop.

Still entirely on-device

Map snapshots, contour tracing, gradient stroking, and video encoding all run locally — the same on-device model as the rest of the app. Nothing about your routes is uploaded to render a poster.
